Biography

I am a Second-Class Professor and PhD supervisor at Guangdong University of Technology. I currently serve as Director of the Data Mining and Information Retrieval Laboratory and am a recipient of the National Science Fund for Excellent Young Scholars. I also serve as Deputy Director and Academic Lead of the Guangdong Provincial Key Laboratory of Media Convergence and Digital Creativity. I obtained my Bachelor's degree in Applied Mathematics in 2005 and PhD degree in Computer Science in 2010, both from South China University of Technology. My research focuses on the theories and applications of causal discovery, causal learning and deep learning. I have led a number of key research projects, including the National Science Fund for Excellent Young Scholars, the National Science and Technology Major Project for New Generation Artificial Intelligence, and the NSFC Regional Innovation and Development Joint Fund (Guangdong). My awards include the First Prize for Science and Technology Progress and the Second Prize for Natural Science in the Guangdong Provincial Science and Technology Awards, as well as the China Patent Excellence Award. Currently, I serve as Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI), Action Editor of Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR) and Neural Networks, and Youth Editor of Fundamental Research. I also act as an Area Chair for major conferences including NeurIPS, ICML and ICLR.

My research revolves around an unsolved fundamental problem in artificial intelligence. While most AI models can capture correlations between variables, they cannot answer how Y will change when X is altered and what drives such changes. This is essentially the problem of causality, which lies at the heart of reliable decision-making in real-world scenarios including network fault diagnosis, clinical decision-making and marketing pricing. Developing stable causal models for open and noisy real-world environments is a cutting-edge challenge in this field. As one of China’s earliest research groups dedicated to causal discovery and causal machine learning, the DMIR Laboratory aims to address a critical flaw of large models: enabling AI not only to perceive "what the world is", but also to understand "how the world will change as we take actions".

CDFM — Causal Discovery Foundation Model

CDFM is a pretrained foundation model for zero-shot causal discovery. It takes observational data as input and predicts a causal graph in a single forward pass. Its pretraining uses a diverse collection of synthetic structural causal models to learn statistical asymmetries useful for causal structure inference.

01Zero-shot graph inference

One pretrained checkpoint is designed to work across different sample sizes and numbers of variables, without task-specific fine-tuning.

02Broad mechanism coverage

The project README reports evaluation across 15 mechanism families and real-world benchmarks.

03Missing-value imputation

A built-in imputation head provides an additional data-completion capability alongside graph discovery.

QUICK STARTpip install cdfm-base
from cdfm import CDFM

model = CDFM.from_pretrained("DMIRLAB/CDFM")
result = model.predict(data)
causal_graph = result.adjacency

TSC-FM Benchmark — Time Series Classification Foundation Models

TSC-FM is a public benchmark for evaluating foundation models on time-series classification. Its official site brings together leaderboard results, dataset profiles, method descriptions, capability analysis, and a protocol for transparent and reproducible comparisons.

causal-learn — Causal Discovery in Python

causal-learn is an open-source Python library that brings a broad collection of causal discovery methods to researchers and practitioners. It combines ready-to-use algorithms with modular building blocks for developing and evaluating new methods.

01Multiple discovery paradigms

Includes constraint-based, score-based, functional causal model, hidden causal representation, permutation-based, and Granger-causality methods.

02Reusable research components

Provides independence tests, score functions, graph operations, and evaluation utilities for method development.

03Research and learning resource

Offers Python APIs, examples, documentation, and a peer-reviewed JMLR software paper.

INSTALLpip install causal-learn
from causallearn.search.ConstraintBased.PC import pc
from causallearn.utils.cit import fisherz

cg = pc(data, 0.05, fisherz)
cg.draw_pydot_graph()
